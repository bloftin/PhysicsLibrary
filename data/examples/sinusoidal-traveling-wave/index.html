Reading a traveling wave

A pattern travels; each marked point stays at its x

For this transverse-displacement illustration, material points oscillate up and down at fixed horizontal coordinates. The waveform moves through those points. A crest is a recognizable phase feature, not a particle being carried along the curve. Horizontal distance is in meters; vertical displacement is in centimeters, so the drawing is not a same-scale picture of a particular string.

With k > 0 and ω > 0, kx − ωt + φ moves toward +x, while kx + ωt + φ moves toward −x. Changing φ shifts the initial cycle but does not reverse propagation.

Space and time answer different questions

A snapshot u(x, t0) varies position while holding time fixed. Its spatial repeat length is λ = 2π/k. A record u(x0, t) holds position fixed and varies time. Its temporal repeat interval is T = 2π/ω, and f = 1/T.

A quarter-period advance shifts any given crest by a quarter-wavelength in the chosen direction. The gold crest keeps the same identity even after it leaves the displayed window; it is not wrapped back to the other side. One local time history alone need not reveal direction: at suitable phases, opposite-moving waves can produce the same record.

Phase is an angle, not a distance or a time

The complete phase is θ(x,t) = kx − ωt + φ for rightward propagation. Each contribution is an angle: (rad/m)×m, (rad/s)×s, and φ in radians. Adding them is dimensionally consistent. The amplitude lies outside the trigonometric function and does not set the spatial or temporal repeat interval.

Sine and cosine are equivalent conventions because cos θ = sin(θ + π/2). Switching the displayed form adjusts φ by 90° to preserve the physical field. The numerical phase then changes, but the disturbance, crests, direction and measured periods do not.

The article's worked events

Example 1 has A = 0.030 m, k = 4π rad/m, ω = 10π rad/s and φ = π/6. It therefore has λ = 0.50 m, T = 0.20 s and f = 5 Hz, and the minus temporal sign means rightward motion.

Example 2 uses u = 2 cos(πx − 2πt + π/3) cm. At x = 1 m and t = 0.25 s, θ = 5π/6 and u = −√3 cm, approximately −1.732 cm. At zero amplitude, the field is flat: λ, T, direction and phase remain nominal generator settings, with no observable crest or smallest spatial/temporal repeat.

WM07 is kinematic. These are exact prescribed sinusoids, not simulated masses, a force-derived string model, an energy-transport calculation or a numerical wave-equation solver. Constant-phase tracking connects to WM08; wave-speed derivations and medium dynamics belong to the later articles.
