From local electrons to traveling energy

Receive: a wave drives a collective response

An incident electric field pushes mobile charges. The component along the wire matters; a perpendicular polarization gives zero coupling in this ideal dipole mode. The magnetic field completes the electromagnetic wave, but the leading receive coupling shown here is electric-dipole coupling.

The incident wave is replaced at the circuit port by an equivalent EMF: Voc = Epeak(L/2)sinθ cosψ, with a chosen loop polarity. A load and the antenna impedance determine current. Voc is an equivalent source voltage, not the voltage actually appearing across a loaded receiver.

Electrons stay local

The cyan markers move around nearby equilibrium positions; the positive lattice remains fixed. Electron drift is opposite conventional current. Neither the markers nor electrons jump off the wire to carry a radio signal across empty space. Electromagnetic fields transport energy.

Microscopic displacements are enlarged by the stated gain, which adapts between operating points. Therefore compare different settings using the actual excursion and current readings, not marker travel alone. Motion follows the collective current; it does not depict random thermal motion, collisions or ballistic electron trajectories.

Current makes charge accumulate

Current is largest at the feed and falls linearly to zero at each tip in this prescribed short-dipole mode. Charge conservation gives dqupper/dt = Ifeed, with equal opposite charge on the lower arm. The upper-arm charge lags the current by a quarter-cycle. When current is momentarily zero, the charge imbalance can be largest.

The displayed triangular current gives a uniform incremental line charge on each arm, except at the idealized feed. Real surface-charge distributions depend on geometry. Only the feed-associated dipole mode is shown, not the full open-circuit polarization or structural scattering of a real wire.

Transmit: the generator supplies the energy

The generator drives alternating current and charge separation. Their changing dipole moment produces outgoing radiation. The radiating far field is transverse, falls as 1/r, and arrives after a delay r/c. Its magnetic field is in phase with E, with B = E/c; the Poynting vector points outward.

The wave-field view draws only the radiation zone at r = 2, 3 and 4 wavelengths in transmit mode. It does not extend the far-field formula into the feed or represent the reactive near field. The close-up and radiation view have different spatial scales.

Tuning and matching are different

A short wire has predominantly capacitive reactance. This lab approximates it by C = 10 pF and adds an optional lossless series inductor. At f₀ their reactances cancel. A suitable resistive load then maximizes power transfer; tuning alone does not guarantee a match.

The frequency plot samples the steady-state response near resonance. Parameter changes jump between steady states; switching transients and ringing are not integrated. The L/C energy readout is energy in those equivalent elements, not a calculation of all electromagnetic near-field energy.

Radiation resistance is not a heater

Rrad is defined so that average radiated power is Ipeak²Rrad/2. It is not a physical resistor dissipating heat. Only Rloss represents antenna heating. In receive mode, part of the extracted power is re-radiated by the driven antenna mode and part reaches the load.

Power-bar values are cycle averages. They must not be read as the instantaneous Poynting flux at every radius. For a lossless conjugate-matched short dipole, the mode re-radiates as much average power as the load receives. Effective aperture can exceed the wire's geometric cross section; it is a wave-coupling area, not a literal collecting surface.

Model: thin, center-fed, electrically short dipole in vacuum, L/λ < 0.1 across all controls; linearly polarized monochromatic illumination; prescribed triangular current. No full-wave boundary solve, mutual coupling, feed-line radiation, conductor skin-effect calculation, nonlinear material, modulation or antenna array. Assumed carrier density 8.5 × 10²⁸ m⁻³ and effective area 1 mm² are illustrative drift inputs, not an RF current-penetration prediction.
